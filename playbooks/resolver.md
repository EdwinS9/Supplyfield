# Resolver playbook (version 0)

## Principles

- Act on the highest net value first. Use the planner's marginal numbers, never your own arithmetic.
- A fix that only pays off together with another one (look at its standalone numbers) must be requested together with that other fix.
- Do not spend to protect copies that another shortage will block anyway. Check which part binds next.
- Money buys time only before a deadline. Compare a fix's arrival with the hour the part runs out.
- Prefer free fixes (pick-ups, concessions, confirmations) over spending, when they save the same copies.
- The plan only changes the warehouse through an action. When two products share a part that is worth money, request the order in which they get it (`set_priority`, highest value per copy first). It is free and always worth it.
- Ask when information is missing. A part worth money with no supplier offer gets one question per supplier ("finished stock you can ship now?"). A shipment due before the deadline with an unconfirmed quantity gets one question to its supplier. Ask each topic once.
- Every request to a person costs attention. Bundle, do not repeat, and do not ask what the data already says.

## When to act alone

- The candidate is marked worth it, its cost is inside the tenant's autonomy limits, and nothing in `needs` is unknown.
- The deadline is close and waiting would lose the fix (an offer that expires, a part that runs out within hours).

## When to escalate

- The candidate needs a signature or approval that only a person can give.
- Two candidates compete for the same budget or the same shared part and the numbers are close.
- The candidate is not worth it alone but is the only way to protect a high-value product.

## Checks

- Is the offer still valid, and will the goods arrive before the part runs out?
- Does another part run out right after this one? Then the marginal saving is small.
- Is a shipment really on time? Ask before assuming it is, when its status is old.
- Does a request already exist for the same thing? Do not repeat it.

## Message style

- Plain words, short sentences. Say what you need, by when, and how many copies depend on it.
- One question per message. Name the shipment, order or batch.
- Give the reason in one sentence with the numbers the planner produced.
